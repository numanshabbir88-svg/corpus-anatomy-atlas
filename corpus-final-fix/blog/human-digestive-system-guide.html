Human Digestive System: Organs, Process & Function

Digestive System · Stomach · Intestines · Updated November 2026
Stomach Small intestine Liver Peristalsis

The human digestive system is a continuous tube from mouth to anus, supported by accessory organs (liver, pancreas, gallbladder). Its job is to ingest food, break it down mechanically and chemically, absorb nutrients, and eliminate waste. This guide covers the alimentary canal, accessory organs, and the digestive process.

1. Alimentary Canal (GI Tract)

OrganFunction
MouthMechanical digestion (chewing); chemical digestion begins with salivary amylase.
PharynxPassageway for food and air; triggers swallowing.
EsophagusTransports food to stomach via peristalsis.
StomachChurns food; secretes acid and pepsin for protein digestion.
Small intestineMajor site of digestion and nutrient absorption (duodenum, jejunum, ileum).
Large intestineAbsorbs water and electrolytes; forms feces (cecum, colon, rectum, anal canal).

2. Accessory Digestive Organs

  • Liver: Produces bile; processes nutrients; detoxifies substances.
  • Gallbladder: Stores and concentrates bile; releases it into the duodenum.
  • Pancreas: Secretes digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase, proteases) and bicarbonate; also has endocrine function (insulin, glucagon).
Digestive path:
Mouth → Esophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus
(Accessory: Liver → Gallbladder → Bile ducts → Duodenum; Pancreas → Pancreatic duct → Duodenum)

3. The Digestive Process

  • Ingestion: Taking food into the mouth.
  • Mechanical digestion: Chewing and stomach churning break food into smaller pieces.
  • Chemical digestion: Enzymes break down macromolecules (carbs, proteins, fats).
  • Absorption: Nutrients pass through the intestinal wall into blood/lymph (mainly in small intestine).
  • Defecation: Elimination of indigestible waste.

4. Peristalsis

Peristalsis is the wave-like contraction of smooth muscle in the GI wall that pushes food forward, regardless of body position.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is the alimentary canal?
The continuous tube from mouth to anus including mouth, pharynx, esophagus, stomach, small intestine, and large intestine.
Q2. Where does most nutrient absorption occur?
In the small intestine, especially the jejunum and ileum.
Q3. What does the liver produce for digestion?
The liver produces bile, which emulsifies fats.
Q4. What is peristalsis?
Wave-like contractions of smooth muscle that push food through the digestive tract.
Q5. What are accessory digestive organs?
The liver, gallbladder, and pancreas — they aid digestion but food does not pass through them.
