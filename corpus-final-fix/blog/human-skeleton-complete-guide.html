Human Skeleton: 206 Bones, Axial & Appendicular Divisions

Skeletal System · Bones & Joints · Updated November 2026
Axial skeleton Appendicular skeleton Joints Bone marrow

The adult human skeleton consists of 206 named bones that provide structure, protect organs, enable movement, produce blood cells, and store minerals. The skeleton is divided into two main parts: the axial skeleton (80 bones) and the appendicular skeleton (126 bones). This guide explains both divisions, major bones, joint types, and bone histology.

1. Axial Skeleton (80 bones)

The axial skeleton forms the central axis of the body:

  • Skull (22 bones): 8 cranial bones (frontal, parietal x2, temporal x2, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid) + 14 facial bones (maxilla x2, zygomatic x2, mandible, nasal x2, etc.)
  • Vertebral column (26 bones): 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 1 sacrum (5 fused), 1 coccyx (4 fused)
  • Thoracic cage (25 bones): Sternum (1) + 24 ribs (12 pairs)
  • Auditory ossicles (6): Malleus, incus, stapes (2 in each ear)
  • Hyoid bone (1): U-shaped bone in the neck supporting the tongue

2. Appendicular Skeleton (126 bones)

The appendicular skeleton includes the limbs and their girdles:

  • Pectoral girdle (4 bones): Clavicle (2) + scapula (2)
  • Upper limbs (60 bones): Humerus, radius, ulna, carpals (8), metacarpals (5), phalanges (14) — per arm
  • Pelvic girdle (2 bones): Hip bones (ossa coxae, 2)
  • Lower limbs (60 bones): Femur, patella, tibia, fibula, tarsals (7), metatarsals (5), phalanges (14) — per leg
Quick count:
Axial: 80 · Appendicular: 126 · Total adult: 206 bones
(Infants are born with ~270 bones; many fuse during growth.)

3. Bone Classification by Shape

Short bonesCube-shaped; provide stabilityCarpals, tarsals
Flat bonesThin, flattened; protect organsSkull bones, sternum, ribs
Irregular bonesComplex shapes; varied functionVertebrae, sacrum, mandible
Sesamoid bonesEmbedded in tendons; reduce frictionPatella (kneecap)

4. Joints (Articulations)

  • Fibrous joints (immovable): Sutures of the skull
  • Cartilaginous joints (slightly movable): Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis
  • Synovial joints (freely movable): Hinge (elbow, knee), ball-and-socket (shoulder, hip), pivot (atlanto-axial), gliding (carpals)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How many bones are in the adult human body?
206 named bones in the adult skeleton.
Q2. What is the axial skeleton?
The axial skeleton (80 bones) includes the skull, vertebral column, and thoracic cage — the central axis.
Q3. What is the appendicular skeleton?
The appendicular skeleton (126 bones) includes the limbs and their girdles (pectoral and pelvic).
Q4. Which bone is the kneecap?
The patella is a sesamoid bone in the knee tendon.
Q5. What are the three types of joints by movement?
Fibrous (immovable), cartilaginous (slightly movable), and synovial (freely movable).
