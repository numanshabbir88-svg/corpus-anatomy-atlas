12 Cranial Nerves (CN I–XII): Complete Anatomy Guide

Neuroanatomy · Cranial Nerves · Updated November 2026
Olfactory Optic Oculomotor Vagus Facial

The cranial nerves are 12 paired nerves that emerge directly from the brain (including the brainstem) and primarily innervate the head and neck. Unlike spinal nerves, they do not pass through the spinal cord. This guide covers all 12 cranial nerves in order — CN I to CN XII — with their names, functions, and clinical testing.

2. Sensory vs Motor vs Mixed

  • Sensory only (3): CN I (olfactory), CN II (optic), CN VIII (vestibulocochlear)
  • Motor only (4): CN III (oculomotor), CN IV (trochlear), CN VI (abducens), CN XII (hypoglossal)
  • Mixed (5): CN V (trigeminal), CN VII (facial), CN IX (glossopharyngeal), CN X (vagus), CN XI (accessory)

3. Clinical Testing (Quick Overview)

  • CN I: Ask patient to identify familiar scents (coffee, soap) with eyes closed.
  • CN II: Test visual acuity with a Snellen chart; check visual fields.
  • CN III, IV, VI: Check extraocular movements ("follow my finger" in six cardinal directions).
  • CN V: Test facial sensation (light touch) and jaw strength (clench teeth).
  • CN VII: Ask patient to smile, raise eyebrows, close eyes tightly.
  • CN VIII: Rinne and Weber hearing tests; check balance.
  • CN IX, X: Check gag reflex; observe palate elevation when saying "ah".
  • CN XI: Ask patient to shrug shoulders and turn head against resistance.
  • CN XII: Ask patient to protrude and move the tongue side to side.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How many cranial nerves are there?
There are 12 pairs of cranial nerves, numbered CN I through CN XII.
Q2. Which cranial nerves are sensory only?
CN I (olfactory), CN II (optic), and CN VIII (vestibulocochlear) are sensory only.
Q3. Which cranial nerve controls most eye movements?
CN III (oculomotor) controls most eye movements, pupil constriction, and eyelid elevation.
Q4. Which cranial nerve is responsible for hearing and balance?
CN VIII (vestibulocochlear) handles both hearing and balance.
Q5. What is the vagus nerve (CN X) known for?
CN X (vagus) provides parasympathetic supply to the thorax and abdomen, and controls voice and swallowing.
