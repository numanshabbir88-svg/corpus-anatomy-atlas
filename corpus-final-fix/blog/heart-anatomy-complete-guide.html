The Human Heart: Chambers, Valves & Blood Flow

Circulatory System · Heart · Updated November 2026
Heart chambers Valves Coronary circulation Electrical conduction

The human heart is a muscular organ about the size of a fist, located slightly left of the center of the chest. It works as two synchronized pumps in one: the right side sends blood to the lungs, and the left side pumps oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body. This guide explains the heart's chambers, valves, blood supply, and electrical system.

1. Four Chambers

  • Right atrium: Receives deoxygenated blood from the body via the superior and inferior vena cava.
  • Right ventricle: Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery.
  • Left atrium: Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs via the pulmonary veins.
  • Left ventricle: Pumps oxygenated blood to the entire body through the aorta. It has the thickest muscular wall because it generates the highest pressure.

2. Four One-Way Valves

  • Tricuspid valve: Between right atrium and right ventricle.
  • Pulmonary valve: Between right ventricle and pulmonary artery.
  • Mitral (bicuspid) valve: Between left atrium and left ventricle.
  • Aortic valve: Between left ventricle and aorta.

These valves open and close with each heartbeat to prevent backflow of blood, creating the familiar "lub-dub" sound.

Blood flow path:
Body → Vena cava → Right atrium → Tricuspid → Right ventricle → Pulmonary valve → Lungs → Pulmonary veins → Left atrium → Mitral → Left ventricle → Aortic → Aorta → Body

3. Coronary Circulation

The heart muscle (myocardium) needs its own blood supply. The coronary arteries branch from the aorta and wrap around the heart to deliver oxygenated blood to the cardiac muscle itself. Blockage of these arteries can cause a heart attack (myocardial infarction).

4. Electrical Conduction System

  • SA node (sinoatrial node): The natural pacemaker in the right atrium; initiates each heartbeat.
  • AV node (atrioventricular node): Delays the signal briefly so the atria can empty into the ventricles.
  • Bundle of His & Purkinje fibers: Carry the electrical impulse through the ventricles, causing them to contract and pump blood.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How many chambers does the heart have?
The heart has four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, and left ventricle.
Q2. What causes the lub-dub heart sound?
The closing of heart valves — AV valves (lub) then semilunar valves (dub) — creates the sound.
Q3. Which side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs?
The right side (right atrium and right ventricle) pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs via the pulmonary artery.
Q4. What are coronary arteries?
Coronary arteries branch from the aorta to supply oxygenated blood to the heart muscle itself.
Q5. What is the SA node?
The sinoatrial (SA) node is the heart's natural pacemaker located in the right atrium.
