Male Reproductive System: Anatomy, Organs & Function

Male Reproductive · Testes · Sperm · Updated November 2026
Testes Sperm Testosterone Penis

The male reproductive system produces sperm (male gametes), delivers them to the female reproductive tract, and secretes sex hormones — primarily testosterone. It includes external organs (penis, scrotum) and internal organs (testes, epididymis, vas deferens, prostate, seminal vesicles). This guide covers the anatomy, physiology, and common questions about the male reproductive system.

1. External Organs

  • Penis: External organ for urination and sexual intercourse; contains erectile tissue.
  • Scrotum: Sac of skin and muscle that holds the testes outside the body, keeping them cooler than core body temperature for sperm production.

2. Internal Organs

OrganFunction
Testes (2)Produce sperm and testosterone; located in scrotum.

Sperm pathway:
Testes → Epididymis → Vas deferens → Ejaculatory duct → Urethra → External orifice

3. Spermatogenesis

Spermatogenesis is the process of sperm production in the seminiferous tubules of the testes. It takes about 64–74 days from stem cell to mature sperm. Sperm production requires temperature about 2–3°C below normal body temperature — which is why testes are in the scrotum.

4. Testosterone

Produced by Leydig cells in the testes, testosterone drives male secondary sexual characteristics (facial hair, deep voice, muscle mass), libido, and sperm production. It is regulated by the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis (GnRH → LH/FSH → testosterone).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is the main function of the male reproductive system?
The male reproductive system produces sperm (male gametes), delivers semen during ejaculation, and secretes testosterone — the primary male sex hormone responsible for sperm production and secondary sexual characteristics.
Q2. Where are sperm produced and stored?
Sperm are produced in the seminiferous tubules of the testes and stored/matured in the epididymis before ejaculation.
Q3. Why are testes located outside the body in the scrotum?
Spermatogenesis requires a temperature about 2–3°C below normal body temperature. The scrotum keeps the testes cooler, which is essential for viable sperm production.
Q4. What is semen made of?
Semen is a mixture of sperm (from testes), seminal vesicle fluid (fructose-rich, most of volume), prostate fluid (alkaline, protects sperm), and bulbourethral gland fluid (lubrication).
Q5. What role does the prostate gland play?
The prostate secretes a milky, alkaline fluid that makes up part of semen. This fluid nourishes sperm, helps neutralize vaginal acidity, and aids sperm motility during ejaculation.
