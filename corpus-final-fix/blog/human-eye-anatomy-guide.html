Human Eye: Anatomy, Layers & How We See

Sensory System · Eye · Vision · Updated November 2026
Retina Cornea Lens Optic nerve

The human eye is a sophisticated optical organ that converts light into neural signals, enabling vision. Often called the "camera of the body," it focuses light onto the retina, where photoreceptor cells (rods and cones) trigger signals sent to the brain via the optic nerve. This guide covers the eye's three layers, internal structures, and the visual pathway.

1. Three Layers of the Eyeball

LayerNameKey StructuresFunction
Outer (fibrous)Tunica fibrosaCornea, scleraProtection and shape; cornea refracts light
Middle (vascular)Tunica vasculosa (uvea)Iris, ciliary body, choroidBlood supply; iris controls pupil size
Inner (nervous)Tunica interna (retina)Photoreceptors, bipolar cells, ganglion cellsConverts light to neural signals

2. Key Internal Structures

  • Cornea: Transparent front surface; does ~2/3 of light refraction.
  • Iris: Colored part; adjusts pupil diameter to control light entry.
  • Pupil: Central opening; lets light into the eye.
  • Lens: Fine-focuses light onto the retina; changes shape via ciliary muscles (accommodation).
  • Vitreous humor: Gel-like substance filling the main cavity; maintains shape.
  • Retina: Contains rods (dim light) and cones (color, detail).
  • Macula & fovea: Central retina for sharp, detailed vision.
  • Optic nerve (CN II): Carries visual signals to the brain.
  • Blind spot: Where the optic nerve exits; no photoreceptors.
Light path through the eye:
Cornea → Aqueous humor → Pupil → Lens → Vitreous humor → Retina → Optic nerve → Brain

3. Photoreceptors: Rods and Cones

  • Rods (~120 million): Sensitive to low light; black-and-white vision; night vision.
  • Cones (~6 million): Sensitive to bright light and color; three types — S (blue), M (green), L (red).

4. Common Refractive Errors

  • Myopia (nearsightedness): Image focuses in front of retina; corrected with concave lenses.
  • Hyperopia (farsightedness): Image focuses behind retina; corrected with convex lenses.
  • Astigmatism: Irregular cornea/lens curvature; distorted vision.
  • Presbyopia: Age-related loss of lens flexibility; difficulty focusing near objects.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What are the three layers of the eyeball?
Outer fibrous layer (cornea, sclera), middle vascular layer/uvea (iris, ciliary body, choroid), and inner nervous layer (retina).
Q2. What is the function of the iris?
The iris controls the size of the pupil, regulating how much light enters the eye.
Q3. What are rods and cones?
Rods detect dim light (black-and-white); cones detect color and detail in bright light.
Q4. Where is the blind spot?
At the point where the optic nerve exits the retina — there are no photoreceptors there.
Q5. What causes myopia?
Myopia (nearsightedness) occurs when the image focuses in front of the retina instead of on it.
