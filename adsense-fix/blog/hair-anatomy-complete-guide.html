Inside a Single Hair: Complete Anatomy Guide

Integumentary System · Hair · Updated November 2026
Hair shaft Follicle Keratin Melanin Sebaceous gland

Have you ever wondered what is actually inside a single hair? Although a hair strand looks simple from the outside, it is a complex biological structure with multiple layers, a living root, and supporting glands and muscles. This guide walks through every part of a hair — from the visible shaft down to the dermal papilla — in clear, medically accurate detail.

1. The Hair Shaft (Visible Part)

The hair shaft is the portion of the hair that projects above the surface of the skin. It is made of dead, keratinized cells and consists of three concentric layers:

  • Cuticle: The outermost thin layer made of overlapping, scale-like cells. It protects the inner layers and controls moisture movement.
  • Cortex: The middle and thickest layer. It contains bundles of keratin proteins and melanin pigment, which determine hair strength, texture, and color.
  • Medulla: The innermost core. It may be absent in fine hairs and is more prominent in thick, coarse hairs.

2. The Hair Follicle (The Living Anchor)

Below the skin surface, the hair grows from a tunnel-like structure called the hair follicle. This is where the living part of the hair resides.

  • Hair root: The portion of the hair located within the follicle, beneath the skin surface.
  • Hair bulb: The expanded base of the follicle where the hair matrix cells divide rapidly to produce new hair.
  • Dermal papilla: A small nipple of connective tissue at the base of the bulb. It contains blood vessels that deliver oxygen and nutrients to the growing hair.
Simple cross-section of a hair:
Cuticle → Cortex → Medulla (center)
Surrounded by: Hair follicle → Hair bulb → Dermal papilla

3. Supporting Structures

  • Sebaceous gland (oil gland): Attached to the follicle, it secretes sebum to lubricate the hair and skin, keeping them flexible and waterproof.
  • Arrector pili muscle: A tiny smooth muscle attached to the follicle. When it contracts (for example, from cold or fear), it pulls the hair upright — producing "goosebumps."

4. What Gives Hair Its Strength and Color?

  • Keratin: A tough, fibrous structural protein that makes up the bulk of the hair shaft, giving it strength and resilience.
  • Melanin: The pigment produced by melanocytes in the hair bulb. Eumelanin creates brown/black tones; pheomelanin creates red/yellow tones. As we age, melanin production decreases, leading to gray or white hair.

5. Hair Growth Cycle

  • Anagen (growth phase): Cells in the hair bulb divide actively; hair grows ~1 cm per month. Lasts 2–6 years.
  • Catagen (transition phase): Growth stops; the follicle shrinks. Lasts ~2–3 weeks.
  • Telogen (resting phase): The old hair is shed and a new cycle begins. Lasts ~3 months.
