About Corpus Anatomy Atlas: Editorial Policy & Approach

Corpus Anatomy Atlas is a free, open-access educational reference built to help students, educators, and curious readers visualize how the human body is organized—from surface landmarks and gross organ systems down to microscopic functional units like the nephron, hepatic lobule, pulmonary acinus, and retinal layers.

How to study with Corpus

Three connected steps from visual orientation to active recall.

01 / ORIENT

Locate landmarks on the anatomical plate.

Start with the interactive body map or open any chapter plate. Thin leader lines identify structures that are directly visible in that view, while deeper or microscopic structures are clearly separated as context notes.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Connect gross anatomy to physiology and microanatomy.

Each guide explains regional boundaries, blood supply, innervation, physiological mechanisms, and clinical relevance—supported by 40 dedicated microscopic tissue cutaways.

03 / RECALL

Test yourself with visual and clinical questions.

Reinforce what you have studied using five chapter-specific frequently asked questions, multiple-choice clinical prompts, and spot-the-structure visual rounds.

Editorial standards & sources

Scientific accuracy grounded in public anatomical references.

All anatomical descriptions, directional terminology, and physiological explanations in Corpus Anatomy Atlas are cross-checked against standard open educational and government references, including OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology, NCBI Bookshelf (StatPearls), and MedlinePlus. Our illustrations are educational models created to clarify spatial relationships and are not clinical scans, histology slides, or patient photographs.

We distinguish clearly between structures directly pinned on an overview plate and deeper unpictured structures included for complete systemic understanding.

An educational reference, not medical advice.

Corpus Anatomy Atlas is designed strictly for anatomical and physiological education. It is not a diagnostic tool, treatment guide, or substitute for professional medical care; personal health questions should always be directed to a qualified healthcare clinician.
