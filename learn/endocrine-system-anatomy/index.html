Overview

How this part of the body works

Unlike exocrine glands that empty into ducts, endocrine glands release chemical messengers directly into surrounding capillaries to coordinate metabolism, growth, and stress responses. Every ductless gland on one figure — including the ones you never see: pineal, parathyroids, adrenals and the islets. Within the human body, the endocrine system (Systema endocrinum) is situated in whole body — ductless glands, receives arterial perfusion via rich arterial supply to each gland, and is innervated by autonomic + trophic hormones.

Structurally, the pituitary gland (Hypophysis) comprises pea-sized master gland in the sella turcica. Physiologically, the pituitary gland releases ACTH, TSH, GH, LH/FSH, prolactin, ADH/oxytocin store. The pineal gland (Glandula pinealis) is tiny cone on the dorsal diencephalon. Physiologically, the pineal gland governs melatonin — circadian rhythm. Structurally, the thyroid gland (Glandula thyroidea) comprises butterfly gland on the trachea. Physiologically, the thyroid gland governs t3/T4 metabolic rate; calcitonin. The parathyroid glands (Gll. parathyroideae) are four grains on the thyroid's back. Physiologically, the parathyroid glands governs PTH — raises plasma calcium. Structurally, the adrenal glands (Gll. suprarenales) comprises caps on each kidney: cortex + medulla. Physiologically, the adrenal glands governs cortisol, aldosterone, adrenaline. Structurally, the pancreatic islets (Insulae pancreaticae) comprises 1–2 million islets in the pancreas. Physiologically, the pancreatic islets governs insulin (β) and glucagon (α) — glucose control.

Clinically, regarding the pituitary gland, pituitary adenoma → visual field loss (optic chiasm above). From a diagnostic standpoint, in the pineal gland, calcifies with age; useful midline marker on CT. In clinical pathology involving the thyroid gland, goitre and thyrotoxicosis are common presentations. Medical evaluation of the parathyroid glands also notes that accidental removal in thyroidectomy → tetany.

Released by stretched atrial myocytes.

Scattered hormone cells of gut mucosa.

What it doesGastrin, CCK, secretin, GLP-1.

11

Adipose (leptin)

Adipocyti

Fat as an endocrine organ.

What it doesLeptin — long-term energy signalling.

Clinical connectionLeptin resistance accompanies obesity.

12

Anterior pituitary somatotrophs

Adenohypophyseal cells secreting growth hormone.

What it doesDrive growth and metabolic signaling via GH and IGF-1.

Clinical connectionPituitary tumors may overproduce GH.

13

Anterior pituitary corticotrophs

Pituitary cells making ACTH from POMC precursor.

What it doesStimulate adrenal cortisol production.

Clinical connectionACTH deficiency causes secondary adrenal insufficiency.

14

Posterior pituitary axon terminals

Hypothalamic nerve endings within neurohypophysis.

What it doesRelease ADH and oxytocin made in hypothalamus.

Clinical connectionPosterior pituitary stores rather than synthesizes these hormones.

15

Adrenal zona glomerulosa

Outermost adrenal cortical layer beneath capsule.

What it doesProduces aldosterone under angiotensin II and potassium control.

Clinical connectionExcess can cause hypertension with hypokalemia.

16

Adrenal zona fasciculata

Middle and largest adrenal cortical zone.

What it doesProduces glucocorticoids including cortisol.

Clinical connectionACTH excess can enlarge this zone.

17

Adrenal zona reticularis

Inner adrenal cortical network adjacent to medulla.

What it doesProduces adrenal androgen precursors.

Clinical connectionAndrogen excess has different effects across ages.

18

Thyroid follicular cells

Cells encircling extracellular colloid in thyroid follicles.

What it doesSynthesize and release T4/T3 after iodination and processing.

Clinical connectionTSH regulates their activity.

19

Parathyroid chief cells

Small endocrine cells of parathyroid glands.

What it doesSecrete PTH in response to falling ionized calcium.

Clinical connectionAdenoma can cause hypercalcemia.

20

Pancreatic beta cells

Insulin-producing cells of endocrine pancreatic islets.

What it doesLower circulating glucose by promoting uptake/storage.

Clinical connectionBeta-cell loss causes insulin deficiency.

Questions people ask

Five questions about Endocrine system

Short answers grounded in the structures described above. Use the illustrated guide for anatomical context.

What organs make up the endocrine system?

The endocrine system includes hormone-producing structures such as the pituitary, thyroid, parathyroids, adrenals and pancreatic islets, with other organs also contributing hormones.

How do endocrine glands differ from exocrine glands?

Endocrine glands release hormones into the blood; exocrine glands send products through ducts to a surface or lumen.

What is the pituitary gland?

The pituitary is an endocrine gland in the sella turcica that releases hormones regulating other glands and body functions.

Which structures lie posterior to the thyroid gland?

The parathyroid glands usually lie on the posterior thyroid surface; the recurrent laryngeal nerves ascend nearby in the tracheoesophageal grooves.

What is the function of the adrenal glands?

The adrenal cortex releases cortisol for stress metabolism and aldosterone for sodium, potassium and blood-pressure regulation; the medulla releases epinephrine and norepinephrine for rapid sympathetic responses.
