Vaginal anatomy: wall layers and fornices

A layered fibromuscular canal: rugae, muscularis, fornices, vestibular opening, adjacent urethra and rectum.

Illustrated structures

Read the anatomy, part by part

These 14 structures describe anatomy related to the chapter’s regional illustration. Each entry separates location, function and a clinical point.

01

Vaginal lumen

Lumen vaginae

Potential space whose walls normally lie against one another.

What it doesPassage for menstrual flow, intercourse and birth.

Clinical connectionDistensibility varies; do not interpret the lumen as a permanently open tube.

02

Anterior vaginal wall

Paries anterior vaginae

Shorter wall adjoining bladder base and urethra.

What it doesProvides anterior pelvic support.

Clinical connectionAnterior compartment weakness may be associated with cystocele.

03

Posterior vaginal wall

Paries posterior vaginae

Wall lying anterior to rectum, longer near posterior fornix.

What it doesSupports the posterior compartment.

Clinical connectionPosterior wall defects may accompany rectocele.

04

Vaginal mucosa & rugae

Tunica mucosa vaginae

Nonkeratinizing squamous mucosa with transverse folds.

What it doesDistends and responds to estrogen; no intrinsic mucosal glands.

Clinical connectionAtrophy can cause fragility; moisture derives mainly from transudate and cervical secretions.

05

Vaginal muscularis

Tunica muscularis vaginae

Circular and longitudinal smooth-muscle bundles.

What it doesAllows distension and elastic return.

Clinical connectionAt the introitus skeletal pelvic floor muscles contribute support.

06

Adventitia & paravaginal fascia

Tunica adventitia

Outer connective tissue joining vaginal walls to pelvic sidewall.

What it doesContains neurovascular routes and supports the canal.

Clinical connectionFascial defects contribute to pelvic organ prolapse.

07

Anterior fornix

Fornix anterior

Recess between cervix and anterior vaginal wall.

What it doesAllows motion around cervix.

Clinical connectionBladder is nearby anteriorly.

08

Posterior fornix

Fornix posterior

Deep recess behind cervix near recto-uterine pouch.

What it doesUseful landmark in examination.

Clinical connectionDouglas pouch is separated by thin tissues superiorly.

09

Lateral fornices

Fornices laterales

Paired recesses to either side of cervix.

What it doesAnatomic route near cervical attachments.

Clinical connectionUreter runs near the lateral vaginal fornix under the uterine artery.

10

Cervix projecting into vagina

Portio vaginalis

Cervical tip enters upper vaginal vault.

What it doesCreates the four fornices.

Clinical connectionExamination of the external os requires visualization of this projection.

11

Vaginal introitus

Ostium vaginae

External vaginal opening within vulvar vestibule.

What it doesContinuity with external genital tract.

Clinical connectionThe hymenal rim is variable and not evidence of sexual history.

12

Bladder & urethra relation

Vesica et urethra

Bladder and urethra lie anterior to vaginal wall.

What it doesExplains anterior pelvic support.

Clinical connectionUrethral injury risk depends on operative plane.

13

Rectal relation

Rectum

Rectum lies posterior to vagina.

What it doesExplains posterior compartment anatomy.

Clinical connectionRectovaginal septum is important in surgery and endometriosis.

14

Vaginal lymphatic zones

Vasa lymphatica

Upper vagina drains to pelvic nodes; lower portion to inguinal nodes.

What it doesRoute for local immune drainage.

Clinical connectionDrainage patterns matter in oncologic staging.

Beyond the visible plate

Deeper structures and tissue layers

These 6 additional entries give anatomical context. They are deliberately not assigned a photographic pin on the regional image.

15

Basal epithelial layer

Deep proliferative stratum of vaginal stratified squamous mucosa.

What it doesRenews superficial epithelium.

Clinical connectionAtrophy changes epithelial thickness and fragility.

16

Intermediate epithelial cells

Glycogen-rich cells in hormonally responsive vaginal epithelium.

What it doesContribute substrate supporting normal microbiota.

Clinical connectionCytology changes with estrogen exposure.

17

Vaginal venous plexus

Intercommunicating veins in lateral and deep vaginal walls.

What it doesDrains to internal iliac venous tributaries.

Clinical connectionEngorgement varies with hormonal and vascular state.

18

Vaginal arterial supply

Vaginal, uterine and internal pudendal branches with regional variation.

What it doesNourishes canal and neighboring structures.

Clinical connectionCollateral supply influences operative bleeding.

19

Perineal membrane

Dense fibrous sheet under deep perineal compartment anterior to vaginal opening.

What it doesSupports urogenital structures and muscle attachments.

Clinical connectionPlane matters in pelvic-floor surgery.

20

Posterior vaginal adventitia

Connective tissue plane between vaginal wall and rectovaginal septum.

What it doesSupports separation from rectum.

Clinical connectionDefects can contribute to posterior compartment prolapse.

Questions people ask

Five questions about Vagina — walls & fornices

Short answers grounded in the structures described above. Use the illustrated guide for anatomical context.

What are the layers of the vaginal wall?

The vaginal wall contains a mucosal lining, a muscular layer and outer connective tissue. Its folds and tissue properties vary with age and hormonal state.

Where are the vaginal fornices?

Fornices are recesses around the cervix at the upper end of the vagina; the posterior fornix lies behind the cervix.

What is the vaginal lumen?

Potential space whose walls normally lie against one another. Function: Passage for menstrual flow, intercourse and birth.

Where is the posterior vaginal wall located?

Wall lying anterior to rectum, longer near posterior fornix. Function: Supports the posterior compartment.

What is the function of the vaginal muscularis?

Function: Allows distension and elastic return. Circular and longitudinal smooth-muscle bundles.
