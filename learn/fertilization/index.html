Fertilization in the uterine tube: sperm meets oocyte

Trace the separate fertilization stage from sperm transit and capacitation through oocyte coats, membrane fusion, cortical reaction and a zygote.

Overview

How this part of the body works

After intercourse, sperm travel through cervix and uterus toward a uterine tube; intercourse does not necessarily cause pregnancy. Fertilization normally occurs in the ampulla of the uterine tube, where a capacitated sperm contacts the secondary oocyte surrounded by corona radiata and zona pellucida. Acrosome-mediated events facilitate passage through the coat, followed by sperm–oocyte membrane fusion. The oocyte completes meiosis II, cortical changes reduce polyspermy, and maternal and paternal pronuclear material ultimately establishes a diploid zygote. The plate deliberately shows the earlier contact step only: it does not show pronuclei, embryo formation or a fertilization event inside the uterus.

Questions people ask

Five questions about Fertilization · sperm meets oocyte

Short answers grounded in the structures described above. Use the illustrated guide for anatomical context.

Where does fertilization normally occur in humans?

It usually occurs in the ampulla of a uterine tube near the ovary. The sperm and secondary oocyte meet there; normal later implantation is in the uterine endometrium, not inside the tube.

Does intercourse always lead to fertilization?

No. Sperm transit, ovulation, timing, gamete function and other conditions affect whether fertilization occurs. Even after fertilization, development and uterine implantation are separate requirements for an ongoing pregnancy.

What is the acrosome reaction?

After appropriate sperm–oocyte coat interaction, acrosomal membrane changes help sperm cross the zona pellucida. The contact plate shows an earlier sperm at the outer zona, not the reaction at molecular resolution.

How is fertilization by more than one sperm limited?

After one sperm fuses with the oocyte, cortical-granule changes modify the surrounding zona and help block polyspermy. This is a biological reaction, not a visible colored ring in the image.

What is the difference between fertilization and implantation?

Fertilization combines gamete genetic material into a zygote, usually in a uterine tube. Implantation happens later when a developing blastocyst attaches to receptive uterine lining.
