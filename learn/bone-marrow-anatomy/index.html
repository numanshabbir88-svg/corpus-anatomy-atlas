Overview

How this part of the body works

Hidden inside the honeycomb cavities of the axial skeleton, red bone marrow manufactures hundreds of billions of fresh red cells, leukocytes, and platelets every single day. Cut long bone plus a cellular inset — the organs no eye ever sees without a microscope, highlighted here. Within the human body, the bone marrow & blood (Medulla ossium) is situated in inside every bone, receives arterial perfusion via nutrient + metaphyseal arteries, and is innervated by autonomic fibres.

The red marrow (Medulla ossium rubra) is spongy bone of vertebrae, pelvis, sternum, epiphyses. Physiologically, the red marrow governs haematopoiesis: all blood cell lines. Structurally, the yellow marrow (Medulla ossium flava) comprises fat-filled shaft cavities of long bones. Physiologically, the yellow marrow governs energy store; convertible back to red marrow. Structurally, the trabecular (spongy) bone (Os spongiosum) comprises latticework of struts along stress lines. Physiologically, the trabecular (spongy) bone governs strength at low weight; houses red marrow. The compact bone (cortex) (Os compactum) is dense osteon-based outer shell. Physiologically, the compact bone (cortex) governs bending and torsional strength; calcium bank. Structurally, the endosteum & osteoblasts (Endosteum) comprises cellular lining of all internal bone surfaces. Physiologically, the endosteum & osteoblasts governs remodelling: osteoblasts build, osteoclasts eat. Structurally, the haematopoietic stem cells (Cellulae stirpes) comprises pluripotent marrow stem cells. Physiologically, the haematopoietic stem cells governs give rise to myeloid and lymphoid lines.

Clinically, regarding the red marrow, marrow failure = aplastic anaemia; infiltration = leukaemia. From a diagnostic standpoint, in the yellow marrow, reconverts in chronic anaemia or severe bleeding. In clinical pathology involving the trabecular (spongy) bone, osteoporosis thins trabeculae first — vertebral fractures. Medical evaluation of the compact bone (cortex) also notes that cortical thickening responds to load (Wolff's law).

Marrow precursor downstream of haematopoietic stem cell.

What it doesGives rise to granulocyte, monocyte, erythroid and megakaryocyte lineages.

Marrow precursor producing lymphocyte lineages.

What it doesSeeds B, T and natural-killer cell development.

Clinical connectionB-cell maturation largely begins in marrow.

Erythroblast island

Cluster of erythroid precursors around a central macrophage.

What it doesSupports red-cell development and iron handling.

Clinical connectionMarrow stress can alter erythroid proliferation.

11

Reticulocyte

Young enucleated erythrocyte retaining residual RNA.

What it doesMatures into circulating red blood cell.

Clinical connectionCount gauges marrow response to anemia.

12

Megakaryocyte

Very large polyploid marrow cell beside sinusoidal spaces.

What it doesSheds platelets into circulation.

Clinical connectionLow production can cause thrombocytopenia.

13

Neutrophil precursor series

Granulocytic maturation pathway from myeloblast through bands to segmented cells.

What it doesProduces rapid innate phagocytes.

Clinical connectionLeft shift reflects release of immature forms.

14

Marrow stromal cells

Nonhematopoietic support cells including fibroblastic and mesenchymal populations.

What it doesProvide niche signals and matrix.

Clinical connectionNiche disruption affects hematopoiesis.

15

Osteoclast

Multinucleated cell resorbing bone along endosteal surfaces.

What it doesRenews mineralized matrix with osteoblasts.

Clinical connectionOveractivity contributes to bone loss.

16

Osteocyte

Former osteoblast embedded within mineralized lacuna.

What it doesSenses mechanical load and coordinates remodeling.

Clinical connectionNot a circulating blood cell.

17

Nutrient artery

Vessel entering bone shaft to supply medullary space and inner cortex.

What it doesProvides oxygen to marrow and bone.

Clinical connectionVascular injury can compromise bone viability.

18

Haversian canal

Central canal of compact-bone osteon containing vessels and nerves.

What it doesSupplies concentric lamellae.

Clinical connectionDifferent from porous trabecular marrow spaces.

19

Marrow adipocyte

Fat-storing stromal cell in yellow and mixed marrow.

What it doesContributes to marrow metabolic environment.

Clinical connectionMarrow fat increases with age in many sites.

20

Sinusoidal endothelium

Specialized discontinuous lining of marrow venous sinuses.

What it doesAllows mature blood cells to enter bloodstream.

Clinical connectionBarrier injury alters blood-cell egress.

Questions people ask

Five questions about Bone marrow & blood

Short answers grounded in the structures described above. Use the illustrated guide for anatomical context.

What is the difference between red and yellow bone marrow?

Red marrow produces blood cells; yellow marrow contains more fat. Their distribution changes with age and varies by skeletal site.

What does red bone marrow produce?

Red bone marrow produces red blood cells, most white blood cells and platelets from hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

What is red bone marrow?

Red bone marrow is blood-forming tissue within spongy bone, especially in adult vertebrae, pelvis and sternum.

Where is the trabecular (spongy) bone located?

Trabecular bone lies inside many bones, especially at the ends of long bones and within flat bones; its struts align with mechanical stresses.

How do osteoblasts and the endosteum contribute to bone remodeling?

Osteoblasts build bone and osteoclasts resorb it; the endosteum lines internal bone surfaces where remodeling occurs.
