Illustrated structures

Read the anatomy, part by part

These 11 structures describe anatomy related to the chapter’s regional illustration. Each entry separates location, function and a clinical point.

01

Brain

Encephalon

Control centre housed in the skull, glowing through the translucent scalp.

What it doesIntegrates sensation, movement, thought, memory and autonomic drive.

Clinical connectionStroke risk rises with age — remember FAST: face, arms, speech, time.

02

Thyroid gland

Glandula thyroidea

Butterfly-shaped gland on the front of the neck.

What it doesSets metabolic rate via T3/T4 under TSH control.

Clinical connectionHypothyroidism causes fatigue, weight gain and cold intolerance.

03

Lungs

Pulmones

Paired thoracic organs flanking the heart.

What it doesGas exchange across ~300 million alveoli.

Clinical connectionSmoking destroys alveolar walls → emphysema.

04

Heart

Cor

Muscular pump in the mediastinum, a little left of midline.

What it doesDrives about 5 L of blood per minute through two circuits.

Clinical connectionCoronary blockage causes myocardial infarction.

05

Liver

Hepar

Largest internal organ, right upper quadrant.

What it doesMetabolism, detoxification, bile, plasma proteins.

Clinical connectionCirrhosis scars the parenchyma (alcohol, hepatitis).

06

Stomach

Gaster

J-shaped reservoir under the diaphragm, left side.

What it doesAcid–pepsin digestion; intrinsic factor for B12.

Clinical connectionH. pylori is the commonest cause of peptic ulcer.

07

Intestines

Intestinum

Small and large intestine coiling through the abdomen.

What it doesAbsorbs nutrients, water, electrolytes; hosts the microbiome.

Clinical connectionObstruction and appendicitis present as acute pain.

08

Urinary bladder

Vesica urinaria

Pelvic reservoir behind the pubic symphysis.

What it doesStores urine; stretch triggers the micturition reflex.

Clinical connectionFrequency + dysuria usually mean infection.

09

Breast

Mamma

Glandular tissue over the pectoral muscles.

What it doesMilk production in lobules under prolactin control.

Clinical connectionNew lump + skin change needs imaging.

10

Uterus

Muscular pear-shaped organ of the pelvis.

What it doesHouses and nourishes a developing fetus; menstrual cycle.

Clinical connectionFibroids cause heavy bleeding — benign but common.

11

Ovaries

Ovaria

Paired gonads either side of the uterus.

What it doesOvulation; oestrogen and progesterone secretion.

Clinical connectionPersistent bloating — rule out ovarian cancer.

Beyond the visible plate

Deeper structures and tissue layers

These 9 additional entries give anatomical context. They are deliberately not assigned a photographic pin on the regional image.

12

Kidneys

Paired retroperitoneal organs beside the upper lumbar spine.

What it doesFilter plasma and regulate fluid, electrolytes and blood pressure.

Clinical connectionBoth kidneys belong in the body overview regardless of sex.

13

Pancreas

Elongated gland behind the stomach from duodenum toward spleen.

What it doesProvides digestive enzymes and endocrine hormones.

Clinical connectionNot separately discernible on this whole-body illustration.

14

Spleen

Vascular immune organ in the left upper abdomen.

What it doesFilters blood and supports immune responses.

Clinical connectionA focused organ plate provides clearer boundaries.

15

Adrenal glands

Endocrine glands positioned on the superior aspects of the kidneys.

What it doesRelease corticosteroids and catecholamines.

Clinical connectionTheir deep position cannot be localized on a surface view.

16

Cervix

Lower part of the uterus projecting into the upper vagina.

What it doesConnects uterine cavity with the vaginal canal.

Clinical connectionThe transformation zone requires dedicated cervical study, not a surface label.

17

Uterine tubes

Paired tubes extend from the uterine horns toward the ovaries.

What it doesCarry the oocyte; fertilization commonly occurs in the ampulla.

Clinical connectionInternal tubal details are not visible on a body silhouette.

18

Vagina

Muscular canal from the cervix to the vaginal opening.

What it doesForms an outlet for menstrual flow and a birth canal.

Clinical connectionThe vagina is internal; the vulva names external genital structures.

19

Diaphragm

Dome-shaped muscle separating thorax and abdomen.

What it doesPrincipal muscle for quiet inspiration.

Clinical connectionIndividual muscle attachments require a deeper plate.

20

Spinal cord

Central nervous tissue within the vertebral canal.

What it doesRelays motor, sensory and autonomic signals.

Clinical connectionSpinal tracts are microscopic and cannot be resolved on this overview.

Questions people ask

Five questions about Female body — anterior

Short answers grounded in the structures described above. Use the illustrated guide for anatomical context.

Where are the main organs in the female body?

The brain lies in the skull; lungs and heart in the chest; digestive and urinary organs in the abdomen; and uterus, tubes and ovaries in the pelvis.

Does a female body map show every deep structure?

No. It is a regional orientation view. Detailed chapters explain deeper pelvic supports, breast tissue, nerves and microscopic layers that a single image cannot resolve.

Where is the brain shown on the female body map?

Control centre housed in the skull, glowing through the translucent scalp. Function: Integrates sensation, movement, thought, memory and autonomic drive.

Where are the lungs in the female body map?

Paired thoracic organs flanking the heart. Function: Gas exchange across ~300 million alveoli.

What does the liver do in the female body?

Function: Metabolism, detoxification, bile, plasma proteins. Largest internal organ, right upper quadrant.
