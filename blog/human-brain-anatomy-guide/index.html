Human Brain: Lobes, Major Structures & Functions

Nervous · Brain · Updated November 2026 · 8 min read

CerebrumCerebellumBrainstemLimbic system

The human brain is the command center of the nervous system, weighing about 1.4 kg and containing roughly 86 billion neurons. It controls everything from breathing and heartbeat to thought, memory, emotion, and voluntary movement. This guide covers the brain's major divisions — cerebrum, cerebellum, brainstem — and the functional lobes of the cerebral cortex.

1. Three Major Divisions

  • Cerebrum: Largest part; divided into left and right hemispheres; handles conscious thought, memory, sensation, and voluntary movement.
  • Cerebellum: "Little brain" at the back; coordinates balance, posture, and fine motor control.
  • Brainstem: Connects brain to spinal cord; includes midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata; controls vital autonomic functions (breathing, heart rate, blood pressure).

2. Cerebral Lobes and Functions

LobeLocationPrimary Functions
Frontal lobeFront of cerebrumExecutive function, planning, decision-making, voluntary movement (motor cortex), speech production (Broca's area)
Parietal lobeTop/back of cerebrumSomatosensation (touch, pain, temperature), spatial awareness, language comprehension
Temporal lobeSide of cerebrumHearing, memory (hippocampus), language comprehension (Wernicke's area), smell
Occipital lobeBack of cerebrumVisual processing (primary visual cortex)
Insula (hidden)Deep within lateral sulcusTaste, interoception, autonomic regulation
Key deep structures:
• Thalamus — sensory relay station
• Hypothalamus — homeostasis, hunger, thirst, body temperature
• Hippocampus — memory formation
• Amygdala — emotion, fear response
• Basal ganglia — movement coordination

3. Protection and Support

  • Meninges: Three protective layers — dura mater (outer), arachnoid mater (middle), pia mater (inner).
  • Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF): Cushions the brain; produced in the choroid plexus; circulates in ventricles and subarachnoid space.
  • Blood-brain barrier: Selective barrier protecting brain tissue from harmful blood-borne substances.

4. Hemisphere Specialization

  • Left hemisphere: Typically dominant for language, logic, analytical tasks, and right-side body control.
  • Right hemisphere: Typically involved in spatial ability, facial recognition, music, intuition, and left-side body control.
  • Note: Functions are not strictly divided; both hemispheres work together via the corpus callosum.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What are the three major divisions of the brain?
Cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem.
Q2. Which lobe processes vision?
The occipital lobe contains the primary visual cortex.
Q3. What does the cerebellum do?
The cerebellum coordinates balance, posture, and fine motor control.
Q4. What is the blood-brain barrier?
A selective barrier that protects brain tissue from harmful substances in the blood.
Q5. How do the two hemispheres communicate?
Via the corpus callosum, a thick band of nerve fibers connecting left and right hemispheres.
