Human Kidney: Structure, Nephron & the Urinary System

Urinary · Kidney · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

NephronGlomerulusUreterFiltration

The human kidneys are bean-shaped organs, about the size of a fist, located retroperitoneally on either side of the spine. Their primary role is to filter blood, remove waste products, balance electrolytes, regulate blood pressure, and produce urine. This guide covers kidney anatomy, the nephron (functional unit), and the urinary pathway.

1. External Kidney Anatomy

  • Renal capsule: Tough fibrous layer covering the kidney.
  • Renal cortex: Outer region; contains glomeruli and convoluted tubules.
  • Renal medulla: Inner region with renal pyramids.
  • Renal pelvis: Funnel-shaped structure that collects urine before it enters the ureter.
  • Hilum: Entry/exit point for renal artery, renal vein, ureter, and nerves.

2. The Nephron: Functional Unit

Each kidney contains about 1 million nephrons. A nephron is the microscopic structure that performs filtration:

  • Renal corpuscle: Glomerulus (capillary ball) + Bowman's capsule.
  • Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT): Reabsorbs most water, glucose, and ions.
  • Loop of Henle: Concentrates urine; creates medullary osmotic gradient.
  • Distal convoluted tubule (DCT): Fine-tunes ion balance under hormonal control.
  • Collecting duct: Collects urine from multiple nephrons; carries it to the renal pelvis.
Filtration path:
Blood → Glomerulus → Bowman's capsule → PCT → Loop of Henle → DCT → Collecting duct → Renal pelvis → Ureter

3. Urinary Pathway

  • Kidneys (2): Produce urine.
  • Ureters (2): Tubes carrying urine from kidneys to bladder.
  • Urinary bladder: Stores urine.
  • Urethra: Expels urine from the body.

4. Key Kidney Functions

  • Filter ~180 liters of blood daily.
  • Regulate blood pressure via the renin-angiotensin system.
  • Maintain electrolyte balance (sodium, potassium, calcium).
  • Regulate blood pH.
  • Produce erythropoietin (stimulates red blood cell production).
  • Activate vitamin D for calcium absorption.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How many nephrons are in each kidney?
About 1 million nephrons per kidney.
Q2. Where does blood filtration occur in the kidney?
In the renal corpuscle — the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule.
Q3. What is the order of the urinary pathway?
Kidneys → ureters → urinary bladder → urethra.
Q4. What does the loop of Henle do?
It concentrates urine and creates the medullary osmotic gradient for water reabsorption.
Q5. What hormone do kidneys produce for red blood cells?
Erythropoietin stimulates red blood cell production in the bone marrow.
