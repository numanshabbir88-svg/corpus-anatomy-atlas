Human Skin: Layers, Appendages & Functions

Integumentary · Skin · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

EpidermisDermisHypodermisSweat gland

The skin is the largest organ of the human body, covering about 2 square meters and weighing roughly 3–4 kg. It forms the integumentary system along with hair, nails, and glands. The skin protects against pathogens, regulates temperature, enables sensation, and synthesizes vitamin D. This guide covers skin layers, appendages, and key functions.

1. Three Layers of the Skin

LayerCompositionKey Features
EpidermisKeratinized stratified squamous epithelium (no blood vessels)Keratinocytes, melanocytes, Langerhans cells, Merkel cells; stratified into strata (basale, spinosum, granulosum, licidum, corneum)
DermisDense irregular connective tissueBlood vessels, nerves, hair follicles, sweat glands, sebaceous glands, collagen and elastin fibers
Hypodermis (subcutaneous)Loose connective tissue and adipose (fat)Insulation, energy storage, cushioning; anchors skin to underlying tissue

2. Epidermal Layers (Deep to Superficial)

  • Stratum basale: Deepest; where keratinocytes divide.
  • Stratum spinosum: "Spiny" layer; immune function via Langerhans cells.
  • Stratum granulosum: Keratohyalin granules; waterproofing begins.
  • Stratum lucidum: Thin clear layer (only in thick skin like palms/soles).
  • Stratum corneum: Outermost; dead, keratinized cells that shed continuously.
Skin cross-section:
Epidermis → Dermis → Hypodermis
Appendages: Hair follicle, sweat gland, sebaceous gland, nail

3. Skin Appendages

  • Hair follicles: Produce hair; associated with arrector pili muscle and sebaceous glands.
  • Sebaceous glands: Secrete sebum (oil) to lubricate hair and skin.
  • Sweat glands (sudoriferous): Eccrine (cooling, all over body) and apocrine (armpits/groin, activated at puberty).
  • Nails: Hard keratin plates protecting fingertips and toes.

4. Key Skin Functions

  • Protection: Barrier against microbes, UV radiation, and physical injury.
  • Thermoregulation: Sweat cools the body; blood vessels dilate or constrict.
  • Sensation: Touch, pain, temperature, pressure via cutaneous receptors.
  • Vitamin D synthesis: UVB radiation converts 7-dehydrocholesterol to vitamin D precursor.
  • Excretion: Small amounts of salts and urea lost in sweat.

5. Melanin and Skin Color

Melanin is produced by melanocytes in the stratum basale. It protects DNA from UV damage. Everyone has roughly the same number of melanocytes; differences in skin color reflect the amount and type of melanin produced, not the number of cells.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is the largest organ of the human body?
The skin is the largest organ.
Q2. What are the three layers of the skin?
Epidermis (outer), dermis (middle), and hypodermis/subcutaneous (inner).
Q3. What produces melanin?
Melanocytes in the stratum basale of the epidermis produce melanin.
Q4. What is the difference between eccrine and apocrine sweat glands?
Eccrine glands cool the body and are widespread; apocrine glands are in armpits/groin and activate at puberty.
Q5. How does skin synthesize vitamin D?
UVB radiation converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin to a vitamin D precursor.
