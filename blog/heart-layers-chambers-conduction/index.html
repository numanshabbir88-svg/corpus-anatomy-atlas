Human Heart Wall Layers, Chambers, Valves and Electrical Conduction Pathway Explained

Explore the human heart wall layers, four chambers, atrioventricular and semilunar valves, coronary arteries, and the SA-to-Purkinje electrical conduction.

Key takeaways

  • The heart wall consists of three distinct concentric layers: the outer epicardium (visceral pericardium), the middle contractile myocardium, and the inner endothelial endocardium.
  • Deoxygenated blood flows through the right atrium and right ventricle to the lungs (pulmonary circuit), while oxygenated blood flows through the left atrium and left ventricle to the body (systemic circuit).
  • Left ventricular myocardium is 3 times thicker than right ventricular myocardium (8–12 mm vs 3–4 mm) to overcome systemic arterial resistance.
  • Electrical impulses originate in the sinoatrial (SA) node, pause ~0.1 seconds at the atrioventricular (AV) node, and travel down the bundle of His and Purkinje fibres.

The three histological layers of the heart wall

From pericardial surface to chamber lumen: epicardium, myocardium and endocardium

Every chamber of the human heart is enclosed by a three-layered wall that is developmentally and functionally homologous to the tunica adventitia, tunica media, and tunica intima of large blood vessels.

The outermost layer, the epicardium (visceral layer of the serous pericardium), consists of simple squamous mesothelium overlying a subepicardial layer of loose connective and adipose tissue that houses the main coronary arteries, cardiac veins, and autonomic nerve plexuses. Beneath it lies the myocardium—the thickest layer—composed of involuntary, striated, branched cardiomyocytes joined end-to-end by intercalated discs containing desmosomes and gap junctions. Lining the interior of the chambers and heart valves is the endocardium, a smooth endothelial sheet that prevents intravascular thrombosis.

Intrinsic cardiac conduction system: SA node to Purkinje fibres

How electrical depolarization coordinates atrial and ventricular systole

Unlike skeletal muscle, which requires somatic motor neurons to contract, cardiac muscle possesses intrinsic automaticity. The sinoatrial (SA) node—located subepicardially at the junction of the superior vena cava and right atrium (crista terminalis)—fires spontaneously at 60 to 100 beats per minute, setting normal sinus rhythm.

Depolarization spreads across both atria and converges on the atrioventricular (AV) node in the inferior interatrial septum (triangle of Koch). Because AV nodal fibres have smaller diameters and fewer gap junctions, conduction slows by roughly 100 milliseconds, allowing the atria to finish emptying into the ventricles before ventricular systole begins. From the AV node, the atrioventricular bundle (bundle of His) pierces the fibrous cardiac skeleton and divides into right and left bundle branches along the interventricular septum, terminating in subendocardial Purkinje fibres that trigger apex-to-base ventricular contraction.

Questions people ask

Frequently asked questions

Why is the left ventricular wall much thicker than the right ventricular wall?

Both ventricles pump the same stroke volume (~70 mL per beat), but the left ventricle pumps into the high-resistance systemic circulation (~120/80 mmHg) whereas the right ventricle pumps into the low-resistance pulmonary circulation (~25/8 mmHg).

What prevents the AV valves from everting into the atria during ventricular contraction?

Papillary muscles contract simultaneously with the ventricular myocardium, pulling taut the fibrous chordae tendineae attached to the cusps of the tricuspid and mitral valves so they cannot prolapse into the atria.

Where is the sinoatrial (SA) node anatomically located?

The sinoatrial node lies in the upper wall of the right atrium at the superior end of the crista terminalis, anterolateral to the opening of the superior vena cava.

Why is the 0.1-second delay at the AV node physiologically essential?

The AV nodal delay ensures that atrial contraction (atrial kick) completes ventricular filling before the ventricles contract and close the atrioventricular valves.

Which layer of the heart wall contains the Purkinje fibres?

Purkinje fibres travel within the subendocardial layer (deep part of the endocardium) lining the interventricular septum and ventricular walls.
